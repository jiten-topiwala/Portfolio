— Feature · I

An engineer with both hands on the metal.

From Robocon competitions to industrial pilot deployments, Topiwala's work has consistently lived where most engineering doesn't: in the field, under load, with stakeholders watching.

Trained as a mechanical engineer and educated by every robot that misbehaved on a competition floor, Jiten Topiwala designs machines that refuse to fall apart. He learned the craft the way roboticists used to — by drafting, machining, wiring, debugging, and watching his prototypes try to survive the real world.

His work spans the unsexy and the spectacular in equal measure: award-winning surveillance rovers that fold for transport, pipe-inspection robots sealed against pressure, biped walkers debating with gravity, and agricultural autonomy stacks that don't apologize for muddy boots.

Currently the lead robotics engineer at Agmove Robotics, he is architecting ROS2 navigation for sub-20HP agricultural rovers. Quietly, in parallel, he is also founding LiftTerra — a robotics company aimed at the brutally practical problems of construction and maintenance.

What separates him is not the breadth of tools — every roboticist juggles many — but the refusal to choose between mechanical and software disciplines. He does both because robots demand both.

— Featured Project · LiftTerra

The startup born from a simple observation.

Construction sites haven't changed much in fifty years. The same workers, the same back injuries, the same lifting — done badly because no one made a better tool.

LiftTerra is the answer Topiwala has been quietly engineering since 2024. A 50kg-payload autonomous platform with follow-me mode, designed to survive the most punishing sites on earth.

Not a fancy robot. Not a product demo. A working, shippable machine for builders who deserve better tools.
